# Phishing Awareness Training

## 1. Introduction

Phishing is a social engineering technique used to trick people into revealing sensitive information, downloading malicious files, or visiting fraudulent websites.

Attackers commonly use emails, messages, fake login pages, and other communication methods to make their requests appear legitimate.

The goal of this training is to help users identify common phishing attempts and respond safely.

---

## 2. Common Types of Phishing

### Email Phishing
Fraudulent emails designed to appear as if they were sent by a trusted organization or person.

### Spear Phishing
A targeted phishing attack designed for a specific individual or organization.

### Smishing
Phishing attempts delivered through SMS or messaging applications.

### Vishing
Phishing attempts carried out through voice calls.

### Clone Phishing
A legitimate message is copied and modified to contain a malicious link or attachment.

---

## 3. Common Phishing Warning Signs

Look for the following indicators before interacting with a suspicious message:

- Unexpected requests for passwords or sensitive information
- Urgent or threatening language
- Suspicious sender addresses
- Misspelled words or unusual formatting
- Links that do not match the claimed website
- Unexpected attachments
- Requests for money or financial information
- Offers that appear too good to be true

### Phishing Indicators

| Indicator | What to Check | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Suspicious Sender | Unknown or unusual email address | Verify the sender independently |
| Urgent Language | Pressure to act immediately | Stop and verify the request |
| Suspicious Link | URL does not match the expected website | Do not click; visit the official website directly |
| Unexpected Attachment | File you were not expecting | Do not open it |
| Credential Request | Request for password, OTP, or login details | Never share sensitive credentials |
| Financial Request | Unexpected payment or bank details request | Verify through a trusted channel |

---

## 4. How to Check a Suspicious Link

Before clicking a link:

1. Hover over the link.
2. Check the actual destination URL.
3. Look for unusual domains or spelling mistakes.
4. Avoid shortened or unfamiliar links when the source is not trusted.
5. If unsure, visit the organization's official website directly instead of using the provided link.

---

## 5. How to Respond to a Phishing Message

If you receive a suspicious message:

1. Do not click links.
2. Do not open unexpected attachments.
3. Do not provide passwords, OTPs, or other sensitive information.
4. Report the message using your organization's reporting process.
5. Delete the message after reporting it.
6. If information has already been shared, immediately inform the appropriate security or IT team and change affected credentials.

---

## 6. Prevention Best Practices

- Use strong and unique passwords.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Keep operating systems and applications updated.
- Verify unexpected requests through another trusted communication channel.
- Avoid entering credentials through links received in unexpected messages.
- Be cautious with attachments from unknown sources.
- Participate in regular security awareness training.

---

## 7. Quick Phishing Checklist

Before trusting a message, ask:

- Do I know the sender?
- Was I expecting this message?
- Is the request unusual or urgent?
- Does the link lead to the expected website?
- Is an attachment expected?
- Is the sender asking for sensitive information?

If something feels suspicious, **stop and verify before taking action.**

---

## 8. Key Takeaway

Phishing attacks rely heavily on human trust and urgency.

The safest approach is:

**STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT**

Never allow urgency or pressure to bypass basic security checks.

## 9. Knowledge Check

### Question 1
You receive an unexpected email asking you to verify your account immediately. What should you do?

A. Click the link immediately  
B. Reply with your password  
C. Verify the request through a trusted channel  
D. Forward it to everyone

**Answer:** C

---

### Question 2
Which of the following is a common phishing indicator?

A. An expected message from a known contact  
B. An urgent request for sensitive information  
C. A normal software update  
D. A scheduled meeting reminder

**Answer:** B

---

### Question 3
What should you do with an unexpected email attachment?

A. Open it to check what it contains  
B. Download it and scan it later  
C. Ignore security warnings  
D. Do not open it and report the message if suspicious

**Answer:** D

---

### Question 4
What is the safest way to access an important online account after receiving a suspicious link?

A. Click the link in the message  
B. Search for the link on social media  
C. Type the official website address manually or use a trusted bookmark  
D. Reply to the sender

**Answer:** C

---

### Question 5
What is the recommended response when you suspect phishing?

A. Continue interacting with the message  
B. Stop, verify, and report it  
C. Share it with friends  
D. Enter fake credentials

**Answer:** B
